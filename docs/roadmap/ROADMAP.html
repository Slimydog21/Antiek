Antiek — Living Roadmap

One roadmap with two registers: a ratified STABLE bedrock and a memento-proposes / operator-ratifies FLUID surface.

Created 2026-05-27 (SPR-00, Living-Roadmap run). Authoritative on UI / surface-organization. Architecture, §5, §9, §16, and the single-writer invariant remain authoritative in docs/master-product-spec.md under its supersession banner. Governance: docs/roadmap/GOVERNANCE.md.

This document exists because three prior vision artifacts (the master product spec; the antiek-unified integration spec; the four-product Read/Write/Speak/DRW specs) diverged both from one another and from shipped reality on the question of how the product is organized into surfaces. The clearest symptom: the master spec's §16.2 records "No 'four products' framing" as a settled REJECT, while the shipped NavRail.tsx is built around exactly four workflows. The roadmap resolves that not by editing either artifact but by separating what is ratified and load-bearing (STABLE) from what is current-best and revisable (FLUID), and by recording the reconciliation explicitly (see Reconciliations below).

Why a roadmap and not just a patched master spec — the steelman, stated and answered. The honest alternative is to keep master-product-spec.md as the single canon and patch §16 in place (a one-line clarification that "four surfaces over one graph" is fine; only graph-separation is rejected). That alternative is attractive: one canon, no second document to keep in sync, no risk of the roadmap and the spec drifting. It collapses for one reason: the three priors disagree with each other as well as with shipped reality, so "keep one canon" still requires choosing which prior is canon on surface-organization — and the choice that matches what shipped (four surfaces over one graph) is precisely the thing §16.2 rejected in words. Patching §16 in place would mean the operator silently editing a settled REJECT, which destroys the audit trail of why the REJECT existed (graph-separation, not the word "product"). The roadmap keeps that REJECT text intact under its banner and records the reconciliation as a first-class, dated artifact. One-canon does not fail because it is wrong in spirit; it fails because it forces an unrecorded edit to a settled decision.

Interaction model (current-best, revisable)

  • NavRail = exactly four workflow doors (Research / Read / Write / Speak), read from WORKFLOW_ORDER so the rail cannot drift from the taxonomy. Source: apps/reading/src/shell/NavRail.tsx — "the four-workflow content-first rail … Exactly four workflow entries — not 8, not 5."
  • One "More" affordance opens the ProductsLauncher, which holds the honest full inventory (every deep mode) plus operator/governance surfaces (Operator, Trust, Settings, the shared bucket). Everything off the rail is one click in via More + ⌘K.
  • Search / ⌘K and New / project-tree (⌘B) are utilities above the divider, visually subordinate to the four doors.
  • U-04 door contract: a workflow's defaultRoute opens to an action, not an explanation (Research → start a research; Read → open/add a book; Write → start composing; Speak → start a remembrance).

  • OPEN  Notebook surface boundary. The TipTap-based notebook is the literate-analysis linchpin (master spec §5.6, PostHog Wedge 2). Whether it lives primarily under Read, is a cross-surface render target, or earns its own rail presence is not settled here.
  • OPEN  Meta-reading boundary. Where "reading the AI's own trajectory/replay" sits relative to Research vs Read (Trajectory replay is currently a Research mode) is unresolved.
  • OPEN  Payout Goodhart / attribution-gaming. The attribution math (master spec §9.3) and anti-gaming (§9.7) remain a live design fork; STABLE invariant 3 fixes the chain and the legal gate, not the final attribution algorithm.

Reconciliations

This subsection records, with both sides cited, where a prior artifact's words conflict with shipped reality and how the roadmap reads the two together. The master spec body is not edited; §16 text stays intact under its banner, and the reconciliation lives only here.

R-01 — §16.2 "No 'four products' framing" vs the shipped four-workflow NavRail

Side A — the prior REJECT, verbatim (master spec §16.2 "On positioning and strategy"):

"No 'four products' framing. The substrate is the moat (§2.5). Separating products would have meant separating graphs, which would kill the cross-corpus knowledge-graph value that IS the product thesis. The biography wedge, the research workstation, the brainstorming surface, the creation tool — all one substrate. Re-raised in the May 2026 reframing analysis and explicitly REJECTed."
source A: docs/master-product-spec.md §16.2 "No 'four products' framing"

Side B — the shipped usage, verbatim (frontend source):

"NavRail (SPR-04) — the four-workflow content-first rail. … Exactly four workflow entries — not 8, not 5. Everything else (deep modes, Operator, Trust, Settings, the shared bucket) lives behind the single 'More' affordance + ⌘K, never on the rail."
source B: apps/reading/src/shell/NavRail.tsx (lines 18–19, 46); taxonomy at apps/reading/src/shell/workflowTaxonomy.ts

The reconciliation. The two are not in conflict once the object of the REJECT is read precisely. §16.2 rejects separating products — and its own next sentence states why: "Separating products would have meant separating graphs." The thing rejected is graph-separation (separate silos with separate substrates), because that would kill the cross-corpus value that is the product thesis. It is not a prohibition on the word "product" or on organizing the UI into four named surfaces. The shipped NavRail does exactly what §16.2 protects: four surfaces (Research / Read / Write / Speak) are lenses over one graph — the taxonomy maps every mode to one of four workflows over a single substrate, and isWorkflowDestination is the single boundary that keeps the rail to four. No graph is separated; the moat (STABLE invariant 1) is intact. The reconciled reading, recorded as canon on UI/surface-organization: four surfaces over one graph is permitted and is what shipped; four separate product silos with separate graphs remains REJECTed exactly as §16.2 states.

What §16.2 rejectsWhat the roadmap (and the shipped rail) permits
Separating graphs — distinct substrates / silos per product. This kills cross-corpus value and is REJECTed, unchanged. Four named surfaces (lenses) over one substrate/one graph. The NavRail's four doors are views, not silos; the taxonomy enforces one-graph membership.
