Gateway binding, fallback, settlement

Owner: billing/gateway worker · Budget: 7 milestones · Depends on SPR-01

Parent context

ResearchProviderGateway already projects, reserves, marks dispatch possible, sends once, settles, releases, and reconciles unknown outcomes. Its current _hold_key_map is populated after dispatch-time decisions and is not durable across restart. This sprint makes the authority binding part of the pre-reservation ledger truth.

The same persisted binding must govern recovery and receipts. Call-time decrypt occurs only after the dispatch guard, current authority revalidation, and successful reservation; it must use the credential named by the frozen owner binding.

Goal

Make payer and credential attribution durable before money can be reserved, then carry it unchanged through send, reconciliation, settlement, usage, and receipt.

Technical milestones

  1. Extend paid intent/hold schema with authority digest, tagged non-secret credential binding digest/key ID surrogate, payer kind/approval digest, ledger account kind, and owner pseudonym or internal owner FK as required; migrate idempotently.
  2. Make reserve accept the complete binding atomically; remove register_hold_key as authority (temporary compatibility reads may remain shadow-only). Reject same reservation key with any changed binding.
  3. Implement payer-specific funding: BYOT creates no provider-money reservation/debit in the house ledger and uses the owner/action budget envelope plus BYOT usage attribution; house reserves projected cents against the exact approval ceiling and house ledger account. A common product budget may cap either but cannot change payer/funding attribution.
  4. Revalidate route, current credential metadata or house route binding, approval, and manifest immediately before reserve. After reserve and dispatch guard, call the request-local adapter that decrypts the exact selected binding only for send_once.
  5. Bind every fallback rung to its own credential+payer, payer-specific ledger account, and chain ceiling. Unknown outcome blocks advancement; definitely-not-sent releases the same payer hold and permits only the next preapproved rung.
  6. Settle/reconcile using persisted binding after process restart. BYOT success settles only to owner/key usage; house success settles actual cents to the house hold/approval and never receives BYOT key attribution. Prove BYOT cannot debit house and house cannot settle as BYOT.
  7. Emit allowlisted PayerReceipt: receipt ID, request/action, requested and actual provider/model, payer kind, projected/actual cents, hold/terminal state, fallback index, timestamps, balance freshness/status. Exclude plaintext, credential ref, owner ID, endpoint, raw provider evidence, approval token.
  8. Add migrations, concurrency/replay/restart tests, payer-ledger isolation, fallback downgrade tests, receipt redaction snapshots, and compatibility test for legacy holds (read/reconcile conservatively; never invent BYOT attribution).

Likely files: runtime/research_runner/provider_gateway.py, spend ledger/schema modules, BYOT usage ledger, migrations and focused tests.

Rigor — five values

1 · Intellectual honesty

A legacy hold without a durable authority binding is not proof of BYOT payment. Label it legacy/unknown and never manufacture a key receipt from the old in-memory map.

2 · Fairness

Steelman the map: it avoided a core schema change. Reject it because restart, replay, and reconciliation are exactly where billing attribution must remain authoritative.

3 · Rigor

Crash-inject after reserve, after dispatch marker, after provider return, and before settle; every restart must converge without a new payer or fallback rung.

4 · Diligence

Preserve existing route-authority digest, idempotency, dispatch guard, and evidence validation rather than wrapping a second gateway around them.

5 · Defensibility

Document every receipt field and why it is necessary/non-secret; prove excluded identifiers with serialized snapshots and log capture.

Dependencies and out of scope

Requires SPR-01 types and payer policy. Requires existing provider gateway, ledger, route authority, and usage ledger tests. No UI or Write route edits; no live paid request; no redesign of provider billing evidence.
