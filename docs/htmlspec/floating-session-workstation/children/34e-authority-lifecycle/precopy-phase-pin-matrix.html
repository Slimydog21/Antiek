Issuer-owned copy custody

The issuer retains its authenticated target BEGIN IMMEDIATE lease after independently committing the durable copy_prepared journal. Its copy command accepts only that exact committed state hash and no caller row material. Under the same sealed-root transition lock it re-extracts the corpus, requires the signed manifest/audit pins, and classifies the target as either all ten migration tables empty or byte-for-byte exact-complete. Empty executes plain INSERTs in canonical table/row/model-field order inside the retained transaction; nonempty executes observed-target verification without rewriting. Any partial, extra, divergent, runtime, singleton, schema, foreign-key, signature, chain, floor or audit state rejects. The issuer reproduces the signed CopyAuditV1 before COMMIT, commits the target, immediately reacquires BEGIN IMMEDIATE, reproduces the observed audit again, and retains custody through reserve/replay and independent durable-journal verification of copied_epoch0. Only that committed CAS releases the lease. On the initial-store ordinary command channel, a lost target-copy response retries as exact-complete without rewriting; this statement does not authorize reopening a consumed recovery session. A zero-row sealed corpus uses issuer state to distinguish first execution from replay because its durable target image is intentionally identical.

Recovery API override

open_recovery_epoch0 accepts a tagged pin bundle whose fields must match this table and the abort-inheritance rule exactly; it covers all four pre-copy phases plus abort_*. Supplying a value where null is required, omitting a required value, or disagreeing with authenticated root/journal state rejects. It exposes only recover_seal_and_copy and authenticated abort. Crash tests are required on both sides of barrier acquisition, root seal, every external CAS, and every abort-origin class.

The executable admission foundation is Epoch0RecoveryAuthorityPinsV1 plus _authenticate_epoch0_recovery_state_v1: for every pre-abort epoch-zero phase it closes the exact phase/version/issuer-sequence/nullability tuple and pins signed state hash, root/store identity, parent device+inode, target basename/device+inode and all five phase pins. Authentication requires the caller's already-open parent and no-follow target descriptors, canonical signed journal under the pinned parent, exact pathname-to-fd inode equality before and after journal verification, and the pinned issuer verification key. SignedMigrationRecoveryTicketV1 is issued at the issuer's authenticated initial bind and domain-binds that issuer generation, root directory, root manifest, target store, parent directory, target basename/inode, maximum sequence and a random nonce. The complete ticket material is a bearer handoff capability and must not be published or treated as identity by signature alone. SignedEpoch0RecoveryAdmissionV1 domain-binds one exact ticket, authenticated socket peer PID, fresh caller/handle nonces, descriptor mode and the complete authority-pin object. The isolated fixture has a distinct supervisor process: supervisor exit terminates the issuer generation, while initial-store exit or fork revokes that PID's ordinary command channel without killing the issuer. A fresh replacement PID may call only recovery admission; it must present the bearer ticket and all three exact descriptors. The issuer independently reauthenticates those descriptors on first issuance and exact replay, binds cached replay to the same replacement PID, rejects nonce equivocation, and rejects inherited initial-store streams after fork.

FixtureMigrationRecoverySessionV1 is the support-only live-session foundation. After initial-store revocation it opens one explicitly framed duplex issuer channel, locally duplicates the root/parent/target descriptors with close-on-exec, and verifies the issuer PID, ticket, signed admission, replacement PID, nonces, descriptor mode and complete pins before construction. The issuer holds its own independent descriptor copies and reauthenticates them on every correlated command. The immutable handle is process-bound, rejects copying and serialization, performs fork-local cleanup without sending authority-changing protocol traffic, and closes its socket and descriptor copies exactly once. One issuer generation admits one replacement PID and at most one live session at a time. The issuer registers an exit watch for that exact process; while it remains alive, only a byte-identical cached admission may open a sequential replacement socket after response loss. Different PID, nonce, pin or descriptor material rejects, and observed replacement-process exit permanently disables replay so PID reuse cannot inherit authority.

Sealed-to-prepared recovery bridge

session_recover_sources_sealed_to_copy_prepared accepts only the exact admitted sealed-state hash in addition to the common session correlation fields. Corpus rows, audit material, counts, successor fields, timestamps, paths, descriptors, lease hints and fault selectors are forbidden. Under the root transition lock the issuer rereads the signed sources_sealed journal, authenticates the sealed root and corpus, acquires the target BEGIN IMMEDIATE lease, proves the target remains exact schema-only, derives the full CopyAuditV1, signs the unique prepared successor, durably CASes and rereads the journal, and retains the lease for copying. Epoch0RecoveryCopyPreparationCompletionV1 carries both signed states and the audit with every authority-conferral flag false; its production verifier proves the exact origin pins, legal transition and audit binding. The signed admission remains bound to sources_sealed. Descriptor authentication derives effective pins from the cached signed successor and the exact signed durable journal, allowing a lost preparation response to be retrieved by byte-identical exact re-admission and allowing the existing copy command to chain on the same session. The executable integration proof deliberately drops the first preparation response, reopens exactly, verifies the cached completion, then completes copy_prepared→copied_epoch0 and proves target-lease release.

The preparation persistence matrix injects faults after audit and successor caching, rename, parent-directory fsync, and durable reread. After-audit recovery authenticates against the still-sealed journal; the later boundaries authenticate against the prepared journal. Every case proves exact close/reopen, byte-identical cached preparation replay without timestamp or signature regeneration, an unchanged schema-only target, continuous target-lease ownership through retry, and lease release only after copied adoption. These process-local injections do not claim power-loss durability or issuer resurrection. Schema/barrier acquisition, sealing, abort and public recovery remain closed.

Recovery-copy operation-fault matrix

This section supersedes the earlier statement that the target-commit/journal durability matrix is open. A failure-only, one-shot internal hook now plants faults immediately after target COMMIT, journal rename, parent-directory fsync, and successful journal reread plus exact comparison. No remote command field can select or suppress a fault. Each boundary must reject its first command, retain the issuer-owned target lease against a competing BEGIN IMMEDIATE, and expose the exact intermediate journal: copy_prepared after target commit, copied_epoch0 after each journal-visible boundary. Retry requires closing the failed transport and exact re-admission by the same live process. The journal-visible paths authenticate against the cached copied pins, take the root transition lock, lock and fsync the parent, reread the exact signed successor, reproduce the sealed corpus and observed target audit, then release the lease. Certification requires the byte-exact durable copied state, no lifecycle temp files, and successful competing-writer acquisition after completion. These are deterministic operation-fault proofs, not a claim of power-loss filesystem simulation or issuer-process resurrection.

Replacement-process lifetime closure

The issuer installs a temporary fork-and-exit watch immediately after reading a replacement candidate's kernel peer PID and before receiving its open frame or descriptors. A pre-admission event tombstones that numeric PID for the issuer generation, rejects the candidate before publishing any admission cache, and removes the temporary watch without killing the healthy issuer; successful admission promotes the same already-active watch without an unobserved gap and only then publishes the signed admission. Thereafter either event permanently revokes the live session and terminates the issuer generation. The issuer drains the watch after frame receipt and immediately before every command effect, so a forked child cannot bypass the public handle's PID check by sending a valid raw frame over an inherited socket. Executable adversarial proofs cover both boundaries: one child forks after sending the framed open request but before consuming admission, is denied an exact same-PID retry, and then proves that a fresh PID can still open and ping; another forks from an established session and writes a correctly correlated raw ping. Neither may receive a successful inherited ping; the established-session fork additionally requires issuer death. Exact response-loss re-admission remains available only while the original admitted process is alive and unforked.
