Goal

One Island primitive, used identically in the Reader, Research and Writing. It is anchored, persistent and reopenable from a margin mark, shows cost before spend and process while it works, and never navigates away.

From any selection, prefix+a (or the quiet Ask affordance) opens an island anchored to the span. The island hinges out of the anchor edge, the Popover hinge the PostHog grammar allows as its one perspective effect. It never covers the passage. It carries the model picker and an estimate, streams process steps and outcome, and can be kept (collapsing to a margin mark), promoted to a thread tab (a child in the MS-04 tree), or merged (MS-08). With nothing selected, the same island opens as an AskComposer, and you attach documents, notes or threads by hand.

Rigor — operating manual for this sprint

How to execute well. Each value has been adapted to this sprint's specific work.

1 · Intellectual honesty

Calibrate confidence to evidence

Name three honesty risks. (1) Without lane B's thread continuation, a follow-up is a one-shot /thought-partner call with no history (floatMenuActions.ts:209). Say "follow-up is stateless", not "Dialogue semantics". (2) Until #3278 merges, Dialogue ignores model_choice, so the island's model picker must say so on Dialogue rather than pretend. (3) A local-only keep must not be labelled "saved".

2 · Fairness

Steelman the rejected alternative

Steelman keeping three separate surfaces (FloatMenu Dialogue, ChaseThread, Write span actions), each tuned to its mothership, with no convergence risk. It loses: three primitives means three lineages, three state models and three places for the next bug, which is the P0 clause's parallel-substitute failure. It has real affordances an anchored hinge could lose: ChaseThread's live ThinkingStream in a panel the user can drag anywhere, and Write's rewrite-in-place. Who pays: the Research operator's dragging habit, and this sprint's four-host FloatMenu refactor. Record which surface-specific behaviours were kept.

3 · Rigor

Claims are mechanically checkable

Test the geometry rule mechanically: the island never covers its anchor. Compute the anchor's getClientRects() and the island's rect at three widths and after scroll. "Looks right" is not a test. Split anchor handling in two. Re-pagination and re-projection must re-resolve the locator to the same exact/prefix/suffix text, tested with a changed page window. An edit or a re-ingest may lose it, and the island must then show an honest "passage changed" state rather than point at the wrong text. The geometry rule is no intersection at 1280 and 1024, and a bottom sheet at 390, where intersection is replaced by focus trap and return.

4 · Diligence

Read before writing

Before building, read FloatMenu.tsx:418-513 (followUp state), floatMenuActions.ts:209-230, ChaseThread.tsx:99-117, the reading-physics anchored-widgets facet and RegionStore, ContextPicker.tsx:28-88, and the ModelUsagePicker. Also read useFloatMenuSelection (the IA-01 root), Write's Outline.tsx:~605 span-action path, the Reading spin-research call site that navigates to /inv/:id (F6), the marginalia and chase-launcher augmentations (M6), and workspace/tabTree.ts (M7). The island must be assembled from these.

5 · Defensibility

Decisions survive turnover

Put the one-primitive decision, and the list of surfaces converged onto it, in a comment at the top of Island.tsx, with the ground ids (IA-01..IA-15, MISS4/5). The next person who wants a "special island for X" then sees why there is one. Also record the converged surfaces and the behaviours kept under "Decisions made mid-flight" in the handoff, and link the master spec's rejected-alternative row ("a new island store separate from threads").
