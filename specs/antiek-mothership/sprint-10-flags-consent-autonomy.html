Contract facts (THREAD-CONTRACT.md rev 7, signed by both lanes 2026-09-26): these override any older wording on this page.
  • A flag is a question-family event: question.identified plus intent, a server-derived actor, target and reason; flag_id is the question_id. The lifecycle is question.diligence_consented / _declined / _launched / _refused (§1.13). Lists come from GET /flags?intent=&project_id=.
  • Routes: POST /flags, POST /flags/{id}/consent (bound to target + context version), POST /flags/{id}/decline, POST /flags/{id}/launch {consent_id}. Accept is consent then launch behind one press; a failed launch retries with the same consent_id. 409 consent_stale re-asks with a fresh estimate. With an unavailable estimate, consent is not offered (R3-2). A reserved question reads reserved.
  • Admission holds nothing: a launch starts if the run's remaining (cap − spent − held) covers X = estimate × 1.5 or the tier max. The consent copy is exactly "Starts if $X of today's cap is free; it pauses if the cap runs out" — never "held". Only dispatches hold (reserve_dispatch). daily_cap_cents, spent_today_cents | null and held_today_cents | null are on GET /settings/budget; raising the cap is PUT /settings/budget/daily-cap. Refusal reasons: refused_capped, unavailable, failed; a mid-run pause reads stopped with cap_reached or cap_overshoot (R5-3, R5-4).
  • chase_mode joins InvestigationStartRequest, and the daemon's SpawnFn routes through the API as a branch-writing launch (§1.13).

Goal

Flags exist everywhere a user looks at knowledge. Diligence runs only after per-flag consent that shows model, estimate and remaining cap. Results land in threads, the companion and the inbox.

A flag action sits on the island, the FloatMenu, companion entries, thread rows and open-question rows. The consent sheet shows the model, the estimate and today's remaining cap, with Accept or Decline. Accepted flags queue as threads and show their state. At the cap they stop as refused_capped, with a one-step raise. The Autonomy settings panel holds the daily cap and the per-run ceiling. "Keep digging" on a thread exposes chase_mode depth, duration and budget.

Rigor — operating manual for this sprint

How to execute well. Each value has been adapted to this sprint's specific work.

1 · Intellectual honesty

Calibrate confidence to evidence

Autonomy spends the operator's money unattended. Every figure the UI shows (estimate, spent today, remaining) must come from lane B's ledger. If it is unavailable, show "—" and block Accept with a reason. Never show a guessed cap.

2 · Fairness

Steelman the rejected alternative

Steelman auto-diligencing every flag within the cap: less friction, and the operator could raise the cap himself. He ruled opt-in per flag (D4), and the cost of the extra click falls on him. Make that click one keystroke from the flag (Enter accepts the defaults) rather than removing it.

3 · Rigor

Claims are mechanically checkable

Enumerate the spend paths (consent → queue → run → follow-up → chase) and assert for each that the cap is reserved before spend (lane B) and that the UI blocks when the reservation fails. One test per path. Name two further cases as tests: refused_capped (Accept disabled with a reason, plus "raise today's cap") and unknown ledger ("—" blocks Accept). Also assert that chase_mode, chase_value and chase_budget_usd are absent from the start request before consent.

4 · Diligence

Read before writing

Before building consent, read interfaces/research/api/cascade_routes.py:810,1203-1235 (spend-preview and owner-bound approval), CascadeProposal.tsx (the existing approval UI), SuggestedResearch's states (CR-06), orchestration/loop_one/orchestrator.py:2270-2382 (chase_mode), NotebookCanvas.tsx:422-430 (the "will surface as a parked question" copy, MISS9) and ground/lane-a-autonomy-and-craft-baseline.json.

5 · Defensibility

Decisions survive turnover

The consent sheet's copy and its no-accept-no-run rule cite D4 in a comment. The Autonomy panel records the operator's cap as he set it, plus an audit line (from lane B) of each change, so "why did it spend that?" is answerable.
